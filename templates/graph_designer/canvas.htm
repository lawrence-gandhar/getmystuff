The canvas arranges itself top to bottom as you build; drag a node to place it yourself, and Tidy up hands the arranging back. To connect two nodes, click the dot or the branch label a connector should leave from, then click the node it should lead to — or drag from one to the other. Hover a connector to reveal its × and its two end handles. Shift-click nodes to pick them, then Test selection to run only those. Everything below the canvas is this run's log.

To move several nodes at once, drag a box on empty canvas, or Ctrl-click nodes to add and remove them one at a time. Ctrl+A selects everything and Escape clears it. Then drag any selected node and the whole selection moves together, connectors included. Selecting a connector picks up the two nodes it joins. Note the two are different questions: shift-click picks nodes to test, Ctrl-click picks nodes to move.

A connector that runs somewhere awkward can be routed by hand: drag the line itself and it bends through the point you drop it at, up to four bends per connector. Drop a bend back onto the straight line to remove it, double-click a connector to straighten it completely, and note that Tidy up asks before throwing hand-routing away along with your arrangement.
